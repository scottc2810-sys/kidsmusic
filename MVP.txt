
# AI Music Teacher for Kids
## Simple MVP Product Specification

### 1. Product Overview

Build an AI-powered music teacher that helps children aged 3 and above learn music through playful, interactive lessons.

The teacher should adapt to the child's progress, teach through a screen or real musical instruments, and provide friendly feedback using voice, animations, and simple musical games.

**Product goal:** Make learning music feel like playing with a patient, encouraging teacher rather than attending a formal music class.

### 2. Target Users

- Children aged 3–8 years.
- Parents who want to introduce music at home.
- Children learning through a screen, keyboard, guitar, violin, or other instruments.
- Families who prefer Hindi, English, or Hinglish instruction.

### 3. Core Features

#### A. Interactive Music Teacher

The AI teacher should:

- Speak naturally and use short, simple sentences.
- Demonstrate musical notes, rhythms, and melodies.
- Ask the child to repeat sounds, tap rhythms, or play notes.
- Use animated characters, musical games, and rewards.
- Give encouragement and gentle corrections.
- Adapt lessons to the child's attention span and skill level.

Example:

"Chalo, aaj hum ek musical game khelenge! Listen carefully. Can you find the same sound on your keyboard?"

#### B. Multiple Learning Modes

| Mode | Experience |
|---|---|
| Screen mode | Learn through animated notes, sounds, and virtual instruments. |
| Real instrument mode | Play a physical instrument while the camera and microphone observe progress. |
| Listening mode | Recognize notes, rhythms, and melodies through headphones or speakers. |
| Practice mode | Repeat previously learned songs and musical exercises. |

Start with keyboard and virtual piano. Add guitar and violin after validating the core learning experience.

#### C. Camera and Audio Learning

With parental permission, the system can:

- Observe the child's hands and instrument.
- Identify which keys or strings are being played when visually distinguishable.
- Listen to musical notes, rhythm, and timing.
- Compare the child's performance with the lesson.
- Provide immediate feedback when a mistake is detected.

The system should distinguish between an actual playing error and an uncertain observation.

For example:

"That was a lovely try! Let's play the same note one more time."

#### D. Recording and Practice Review

Allow parents to upload or record practice sessions of up to 10 minutes.

The AI should:

1. Identify the instrument and the lesson being practiced.
2. Analyze notes, rhythm, timing, and relevant technique.
3. Identify the child's strongest areas.
4. Find recurring mistakes.
5. Create a short, personalized improvement lesson.
6. Track improvement across sessions.

Do not require the child to complete a 10-minute recording. Short practice sessions should be the default.

#### E. Adaptive Difficulty

The teacher should adjust difficulty based on actual performance.

- If the child struggles, simplify the exercise.
- If the child succeeds repeatedly, introduce a slightly harder challenge.
- If the child loses interest, switch to a short musical game.
- If the child becomes frustrated, offer a break or an easier activity.

Progress should be based on demonstrated musical ability, not the number of minutes spent practicing.

### 4. Learning Curriculum

#### Level 1: Discover Music

- Explore high and low sounds.
- Recognize loud and soft sounds.
- Tap simple rhythms.
- Identify different instruments.
- Play one or two notes.

#### Level 2: Play and Repeat

- Match musical notes.
- Follow simple rhythmic patterns.
- Play short musical phrases.
- Learn basic finger placement.
- Recognize repeated musical patterns.

#### Level 3: Create Music

- Play simple melodies.
- Combine notes and rhythms.
- Learn familiar children's songs.
- Experiment with creating original melodies.
- Begin basic musical expression.

#### Level 4: Play Independently

- Play short songs with less assistance.
- Recognize and correct simple mistakes.
- Follow a musical sequence.
- Choose songs and activities independently.

The curriculum should remain flexible. A child may progress quickly in rhythm but need more time with instrument technique.

### 5. Child-Friendly Teaching Psychology

The teacher should follow these principles:

- Keep lessons short, generally 3–5 minutes for a young child.
- Teach one new concept at a time.
- Use repetition without making practice feel boring.
- Give immediate, specific, positive feedback.
- Allow the child to choose between simple activities.
- Encourage exploration and creativity.
- Never compare the child with other children.
- Never shame, punish, or criticize the child for mistakes.
- Never pressure the child to continue when they want to stop.

Use rewards for effort, exploration, and progress rather than perfection.

### 6. Parent Dashboard

Parents should be able to:

- Create and manage a child's profile.
- Select the preferred teaching language.
- Select the instrument and learning mode.
- Set session duration and practice reminders.
- View completed lessons and learning progress.
- Review recordings and AI-generated feedback.
- Delete recordings and learning data.
- Manage camera and microphone permissions.

Parents should be able to disable recording and use screen-only lessons.

|---|---|
| Development | Claude Code |
| Frontend | React, TypeScript, Vite |
| Backend | Python, FastAPI |

Claude should manage teaching decisions, explanations, and lesson adaptation. Specialized audio and computer-vision components should handle musical analysis and instrument recognition.

### 8. Claude Code Implementation

Use Claude Code to build the application, backend, lesson engine, and integrations.

The MVP should include:

- A simple web application.
- A conversational AI music teacher.
- A basic virtual piano.
- A microphone-based note and rhythm analyzer.
- A camera-based instrument observation module.
- A lesson progression engine.
- A parent dashboard.

The application should work locally during development.

Claude Code subscription access does not automatically mean that a deployed application can use Claude's API without separate API access or applicable usage limits. Keep the AI provider configurable.

### 9. MVP Development Phases

**Phase 1: Virtual Piano**

Build the virtual piano, voice teacher, basic lessons, and adaptive difficulty.

**Phase 2: Real Instrument Support**

Add microphone-based musical analysis and camera-based observation for physical keyboards.

**Phase 3: Practice Recordings**

Add recordings up to 10 minutes, automated analysis, and personalized practice feedback.

**Phase 4: Additional Instruments**

Extend the system to guitar and violin with instrument-specific lessons and performance analysis.

### 10. MVP Success Criteria

The MVP is successful when:

- A child can complete a simple lesson with minimal adult assistance.
- The teacher can recognize basic musical notes and rhythms.
- The system can adjust lesson difficulty based on performance.
- Parents can review learning progress.
- A practice recording produces useful, understandable feedback.
- The application works with both screen-based and real-instrument learning.

### 11. Safety and Privacy

Because the product is intended for very young children:

- Require parental consent before enabling the camera, microphone, or recordings.
- Do not use the camera or microphone without explicit permission.
- Process recordings locally where possible.
- Do not use children's recordings to train AI models.
- Do not expose recordings or personal information to other users.
- Provide clear controls for stopping a lesson or deleting data.
- Keep the teacher's language age-appropriate and non-manipulative.

### Final MVP Scope

Start with one instrument, one virtual piano, three learning levels, Hindi/English/Hinglish voice interaction, and a basic adaptive lesson engine.

Once children can reliably learn and play simple melodies, add real-instrument recognition, 10-minute recording analysis, guitar, and violin.

The priority is not to build a music expert that knows every instrument. It is to build a patient teacher that helps a young child enjoy music, recognize progress, and learn one small skill at a time.
